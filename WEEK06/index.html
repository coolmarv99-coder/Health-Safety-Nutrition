Opening vignette

“The Day Nothing Goes as Planned”

Maya is helping in a preschool classroom when a child trips on the edge of a rug and scrapes his knee. The injury is minor, but the adults respond with calm purpose.

One teacher kneels beside the child and reassures him. Another keeps the rest of the class engaged. Someone retrieves the first aid kit while another checks the incident form.

Later, the teacher tells Maya, “Most emergencies are not dramatic. But if we wait until something happens to decide what to do, we are already behind.”

Maya thinks about fire drills, earthquakes, lockdowns, allergic reactions, missing children, family notifications, emergency cards, evacuation bags, and first aid supplies.

She writes: Calm is easier when preparation has already happened.
