Maya notices the quiet behavior

Mental health is part of the foundation for learning.

A child curls up alone under a classroom table and refuses to join the group. He is not screaming or hurting anyone. A teacher sits nearby—not too close—and says softly, “I’m here when you’re ready.” No lecture, threat, or dismissal. Just calm presence.

Later, Maya learns that relationships, stress, trauma, culture, family experiences, routines, and emotional safety shape participation. Running, crying, hitting, hiding, clinging, refusing, and shutting down are observable behaviors—not diagnoses. Each may communicate a need for safety, regulation, connection, or more information.

Teachers are not therapists. They can create supportive environments, document patterns, partner respectfully with families, consult supervisors or mental-health specialists, and respond promptly when safety is at risk.

What does the educator need next?

Choose the strongest first response. The same behavior can mean different things in different children or contexts; professional judgment begins with observation and relationship.

1. During an unusually noisy activity, Ben curls under a table. He is safe, quiet, and does not respond when invited to the rug.

2. Lina cries at drop-off for three minutes, accepts comfort, and then plays as usual. This has happened twice after long weekends.

Some separation distress may be developmentally expected; note the pattern and successful recovery.

3. For three weeks, Amir has stopped choosing favorite activities, eats less, tires easily, and withdraws from friends across several parts of the day.

4. A child says, “I’m going to hurt myself,” and reaches for a dangerous object.

Maintain supervision and safety while following emergency, reporting, and notification procedures.

5. Maya hears “Keisha is defiant” in a staff note. No specific actions, setting, frequency, or adult response are recorded.

6. After a schedule change, Zoe repeatedly asks what comes next and becomes tense. A visual schedule and brief preview help her rejoin.

An educator can be trauma-responsive without becoming a therapist when…

Observe

Describe behavior, context, pattern, functioning, and response without labeling the child or claiming a cause.

Support

Use safe relationships, predictable environments, co-regulation, choice, and explicit social-emotional teaching.

Collaborate

Partner with families, supervisors, consultants, and qualified professionals while protecting confidentiality and responding urgently to danger.
