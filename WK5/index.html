Opening vignette

“Safe Enough to Explore”

Maya watches children scatter across the preschool yard. One runs toward the slide. Two crouch near dirt looking for bugs. Others turn sticks into magic wands.

At first, the yard looks like freedom. Then she notices the planning behind it: fencing, impact-absorbing surfacing, open space around swings, and teachers positioned so they can see across different play areas.

A child reaches the top of the climbing structure and pauses. Maya worries for a moment, but the nearby teacher stays close without stopping him. “Use both hands,” she says. “Check where your feet are going.”

The child climbs down and beams. Maya realizes that safety is not about removing every challenge. Children need to run, climb, balance, dig, test their bodies, and feel capable.

She writes: The goal is not a risk-free childhood. The goal is thoughtful, supervised, developmentally appropriate challenge.

Safety Scan / Changing outdoor conditions

🔍 Scan the entire yard

Classify each condition. Some require immediate correction, some support safe exploration, and some require more information.

1. A tricycle is parked inside the climber’s use zone.

2. Two educators stand in separate zones with overlapping sight lines and signal when children move between areas.

3. A child climbs onto a broad, low boulder while a teacher remains close.

Consider height, landing surface, footwear, weather, the child’s ability, and whether the teacher can assist.

4. Loose-fill surfacing beneath the slide has been kicked away until hard ground is visible.

5. The temperature is rising, but children are active in both sun and shade.

Check actual conditions, air quality, hydration access, exertion, children’s responses, and program policy.

6. The gate is self-closing and self-latching, and its route remains visible from a staffed zone.
