Safety Is an Active Practice

Welcome, Safety Detective. Preventing injury means noticing conditions, assessing risk, preventing harm, and knowing how to respond—while still protecting children’s opportunities to explore.

Learning focus

Use a repeatable professional-reasoning cycle to examine environments, make supervision decisions, prevent injuries, and choose an appropriate response.

Station 1 · Your detective lens

Learn the safety reasoning cycle

A professional does more than spot a hazard. The complete cycle turns observation into purposeful action.

NOTICEWhat do I see, hear, and know?
ASSESSWho could be harmed, how, and how urgently?
PREVENTWhat change reduces risk before harm occurs?
RESPONDWhat must I do, communicate, or document now?

A wet patch near the water table

You notice a small wet patch. No child is near it yet, but children will soon cross that route during cleanup.

Safety Detective rule: “I found it” is only the beginning. Explain the risk and choose a proportionate action.
Station 2 · Safety Scan / Hazard Hunt

Scan the preschool room

Classify each observation. Some conditions are clearly unsafe; others are acceptable or depend on context. Professional judgment includes knowing when more information is needed.

1. An unlabeled spray bottle sits on a low shelf beside the sink.

2. Open shelves are below adult waist height, leaving clear sight lines across play areas.

3. A preschooler balances on a low log while an educator stands within reach.

Consider the child’s abilities, the height, landing surface, and quality of supervision.

4. A teacher sits with a small group while a tall storage unit blocks the dramatic-play corner from view.

5. A classroom door has a bell that sounds when it opens.

The bell can support listening, but investigate whether the door is secured and whether staff still maintain visual supervision.

Station 3 · Active supervision

Choose your position

The environment changes as children move. Your position must preserve sight, sound, access, and accountability.

Outdoor zones are shifting

Two teachers supervise 16 preschoolers. Six gather at the new sand table, four use wheeled toys, five climb, and one child who often follows distant sounds moves toward the gate. The teachers are currently standing together beside the sand table.

Active supervision: set up the environment, position staff, scan and count, listen, anticipate behavior, and engage or redirect.
Station 4 · Maya’s case file

Complete the full cycle

A fall without an obvious injury

Maya sees Jordan slip on loose mulch beneath the climber. Jordan stands up, says “I’m okay,” and returns to play. The mulch is thin in the landing zone.

Select every action that belongs in a complete professional response.

Cycle check: NOTICE the fall and thin mulch → ASSESS child and condition → PREVENT another fall → RESPOND through care, communication, documentation, and follow-up.
Station 5 · Professional judgment

When “safe” depends on context

Adults carry ultimate responsibility for safety, but removing every challenge can also remove opportunities to develop judgment and competence.

PROTECT
prevent serious harm
SUPPORT
stay close and attentive
CHALLENGE
allow manageable risk
