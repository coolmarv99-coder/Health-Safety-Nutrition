Maya enters the Nutrition strand

A simple snack carries many decisions.

Maya helps set out apple slices, whole-grain crackers, cheese, and water. Behind that snack are nutrients, food groups, portions, family preferences, cost, access, allergies, culture, and children’s sensory experiences.

A child says, “I don’t like apples.” Maya pauses before saying, “Just try it.” The teacher responds, “You don’t have to eat it, but it can stay on your plate while you eat your crackers.” No pressure, shame, or battle—just another neutral exposure.

Nutrition is both what is offered and how it is offered. Food supports growth; mealtime also teaches attitudes, habits, belonging, and trust.

Teachers can encourage healthy eating without creating conflict when…

Offer

Adults plan and serve varied, developmentally appropriate foods on a reliable schedule.

Respect

Children listen to hunger, fullness, taste, and texture cues without pressure, reward, shame, or forced bites.

Continue

Adults model enjoyment, use neutral language, welcome culture, and provide repeated low-pressure opportunities.
